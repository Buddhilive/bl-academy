# My Writing Style

Capture the authentic, accessible, and pragmatic voice of my tech blog posts.

## When to Use

- Writing new blog posts, tech summaries, or dev articles in my voice.
- Reviewing or rewriting technical drafts to match my tone and formatting.
- Creating event overviews (e.g., Google hardware keynotes, developer conferences).
- Summarizing new programming languages, web frameworks, developer tools, or cloud features.

## Style Characteristics

### 1. Tone and Persona
- **Accessible Enthusiast**: Genuine passion for new tech, developer tools, and ecosystem developments without excessive hype.
- **Conversational First-Person**: Use natural first-person commentary sparingly but effectively ("When I experience this...", "I really got amazed...", "Here is an overview...").
- **Pragmatic and Balanced**: State the facts clearly, acknowledge uncertainties or rumors honestly ("It is still not clear about the matter...", "There are several rumors..."), and conclude with a practical take.
- **Supportive Curator**: Eager to point readers to great tutorials, official sources, and community resources ("I recommend to read the full article here", "Enjoy the post;").

### 2. Structure and Flow
- **Direct Opening Hook**: Begin with 1-2 concise paragraphs introducing the central news, update, or topic immediately.
- **Clean Sectional Headings (H2)**: Break comprehensive posts (like event roundups or multifaceted topics) into concise subtopics or product titles.
- **Structured Highlights**:
  - Use bullet points or numbered lists when cataloging tools, companies, features, or product specs.
  - Present essential specs, pricing, and key features concisely without unnecessary padding.
- **Grounded Technical Context**: Explain why a technology matters in simple, high-impact terms (e.g., explaining why Go excels at concurrency or why HTTPS protects visitor trust).
- **Concise Wrap-Up**: Conclude with a clear, grounded takeaway, future outlook, or an invitation to explore a resource.

### 3. Sentence Structure and Phrasing
- Keep sentences clean, informative, and easy to scan.
- Avoid flowery buzzwords, corporate jargon, or overly academic prose.
- Common authentic transitions and phrases:
  - "You may know that..."
  - "Here's an overview of..."
  - "By the way..."
  - "This shows how..."
  - "So this proves..."
  - "Anyway, if..."

## Steps to Draft in Buddhi's Style

1. **Clarify the Core Tech Announcement or Topic**: Identify what changed, why it matters to developers or tech enthusiasts, and what the key details are.
2. **Draft the Intro**: Open with 2-3 direct sentences framing the problem, news, or release.
3. **Detail the Highlights**:
   - For single-topic posts: dive straight into the features, benefits, and architectural implications.
   - For multi-topic or event overviews: use clear H2 headings for each item and bullet out specs, prices, or bulleted feature notes.
4. **Weave Personal & Industry Perspective**: Add brief, thoughtful reflections on developer adoption, real-world utility, or potential future impact.
5. **Sign off cleanly**: End with a practical recommendation, forward-looking sentence, or link suggestion.

## Gotchas

- Do not sound like a generic corporate press release. Keep the tone grounded, personal, and developer-friendly.
- Do not overload posts with artificial fluff or repetitive filler. Keep paragraphs short and scannable.
- Ensure technical details (versions, pricing, compatibilities) are crisp and accurate.
