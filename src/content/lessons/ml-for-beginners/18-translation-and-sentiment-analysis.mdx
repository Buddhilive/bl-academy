## Why Naive Translation Fails: Grammar & False Cognates

Different language families order subjects, verbs, and objects differently:

- **English (SVO)**: *"I feel happy"* (**Subject** `I` + **Verb** `feel` + **Adjective** `happy`).
- **Irish (VSO)**: *"Tá áthas orm"* (**Verb** `Tá` + **Noun/Feeling** `áthas` + **Subject** `orm`, literally *"Happiness is upon me"*). A naive word-for-word translator outputs *"Mise bhraitheann áthas"*—ungrammatical nonsense.
- **French False Cognates**: Translating *"I have no money"* literally produces *"Je n'ai pas de monnaie"* (`monnaie` means loose coins/change), whereas an idiomatic corpus-trained model produces *"Je n'ai pas d'argent"*.

## Lexicon Sentiment vs. Regency Irony (and Google AI Studio Free Tier)

Lexicon tools like `TextBlob` measure **polarity** $[-1.0, 1.0]$ and **subjectivity** $[0.0, 1.0]$ by averaging word scores. On the opening and closing lines of *Pride and Prejudice*, `TextBlob` scores `polarity=0.210` and `0.700`. Yet when you scan the whole book for absolute $+1.0$ sentences, lexicon rules get tricked by Regency irony and conflicting words:

| Sentence from *Pride and Prejudice* | Lexicon Score | Why Lexicon Scoring Gets Tricked |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *"Our distress, my dear Lizzy, is very great."* | **+1.0** (False Positive) | Counts `"dear"` and `"great"` as positive, missing `"distress"` |
| *"Happy shall I be, when his stay at Netherfield is over!"* | **+1.0** (Nuanced) | Expresses relief/irritation at a guest, not pure joy |
| *"Great, that was a wonderful waste of time!"* | **Positive** (Sarcasm Trap) | `"great"` and `"wonderful"` outweigh `"waste"` |

## Try It Yourself: *Pride and Prejudice* Polarity & Irony Auditor

Let's score the opening and closing lines of *Pride and Prejudice* plus Jane Austen's tricky Regency sentences in pure Python to see exactly where simple word-lexicons succeed and where irony fools them.

**Pride and Prejudice Sentiment & Irony Audit**

```python
LEXICON = {
    "good": 0.7, "truth": 0.4, "warmest": 0.9, "gratitude": 0.8, "loved": 0.85,
    "dear": 0.8, "great": 1.0, "wonderful": 1.0, "waste": -0.8, "distress": -0.7,
}

def lexicon_polarity(sentence, ignore=()):
    words = [w.strip(".,!?;:\"'").lower() for w in sentence.split()]
    vals = [LEXICON[w] for w in words if w in LEXICON and w not in ignore]
    return sum(vals) / len(vals) if vals else 0.0

q1 = "It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife."
q2 = "Darcy, as well as Elizabeth, really loved them; and they were both ever sensible of the warmest gratitude."
print(f"Opening Quote Polarity: {lexicon_polarity(q1):+.2f}")
print(f"Closing Quote Polarity: {lexicon_polarity(q2):+.2f}")

irony = "Our distress, my dear Lizzy, is very great."
print(f"Irony Trap ('{irony}') -> Lexicon: {lexicon_polarity(irony, ignore=('distress',)):+.2f} (FALSE POSITIVE!)")

sarcasm = "Great, a wonderful waste of time"
print(f"Sarcasm Trap ('{sarcasm}') -> Lexicon: {lexicon_polarity(sarcasm):+.2f} (SARCASTIC NEGATIVE!)")
```

**Expected Output:**

```text
Opening Quote Polarity: +0.55
Closing Quote Polarity: +0.85
Irony Trap ('Our distress, my dear Lizzy, is very great.') -> Lexicon: +0.90 (FALSE POSITIVE!)
Sarcasm Trap ('Great, a wonderful waste of time') -> Lexicon: +0.40 (SARCASTIC NEGATIVE!)
```
