A single agent with a handful of tools works great for straightforward tasks—like a "mom-and-pop" travel desk. But as soon as your system handles **large workloads**, **complex multi-stage workflows**, or **diverse specialist expertise**, stuffing 25 tools into one prompt creates a brittle monolith.

**Multi-Agent Systems (MAS)** split that monolith into a franchise of specialized agents that communicate, coordinate, and hand off work while maintaining **Specialization**, **Scalability**, and **Fault Tolerance**.

---

## The 6 Building Blocks & 3 Core Multi-Agent Patterns

To engineer a reliable multi-agent system, you must design six building blocks: **Agent Communication** (sharing dates/constraints), **Coordination Mechanisms**, **Agent Architecture**, **Visibility** (logging, interaction graphs, performance metrics), **Multi-Agent Patterns**, and **Human-in-the-Loop** escalation.

| Multi-Agent Pattern | How Agents Coordinate | Ideal Use Case |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Group Chat** | Agents exchange messages in a shared centralized or decentralized room | Team brainstorming, customer support triage |
| **Handoff** | Agents pass task ownership sequentially based on rules or completion state | Multi-step workflows (Flight $\rightarrow$ Hotel $\rightarrow$ Car Rental) |
| **Collaborative Filtering** | Independent specialists score options and combine via weighted consensus $S = \sum_k w_k s_k$ | Investment research (*Industry* + *Technical* + *Fundamental* experts) |

---

## Enterprise Topologies: 15-Agent Refund & 14-Agent Support Systems

When designing enterprise workflows like an e-commerce **Refund Process** or **Customer Support Process**, separate **domain-specific agents** from **reusable general business agents**:

- **Refund-Specific Agents (5)**: `Customer`, `Seller`, `Payment`, `Resolution`, `Compliance`.
- **General Business Agents (10)**: `Shipping`, `Feedback`, `Escalation`, `Notification`, `Analytics`, `Audit`, `Reporting`, `Knowledge`, `Security`, `Quality`.
- **Customer Support Assignment (14)**: Combines `Customer`, `Support`, `Escalation`, `Resolution` with `Feedback`, `Notification`, `Analytics`, `Audit`, `Reporting`, `Knowledge`, `Security`, `Quality`, `Compliance`, and `Training`.

*Rule of thumb*: Prefer a **single agent** when one set of instructions and tools can handle the task cleanly; switch to **multi-agent** when distinct roles require separate tools, permissions, and independent audit trails.
