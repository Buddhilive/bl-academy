Labeled datasets are always scarce, whereas unlabeled text is practically infinite. **Language Modeling** turns raw, unlabeled text into a self-supervised training signal: mask out a word and train a neural network to predict it from its neighbors. As a side effect of solving this prediction task, the network's embedding layer learns rich semantic representations tailored to your exact domain.

---

## CBoW vs. Skip-Gram Objectives

Given a sliding context window of radius $C$ around center token $w_t$, Word2Vec defines two classic self-supervised training objectives:

| Architecture | Input | Prediction Target | Objective Intuition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Continuous Bag-of-Words (CBoW)** | Average of context embeddings $\{w_{t-C}, \dots, w_{t-1}, w_{t+1}, \dots, w_{t+C}\}$ | Center word $w_t$ | Fast; acts as a smoothed context-to-word classifier. |
| **Skip-Gram** | Center word $w_t$ | Each surrounding context word $w_{t+j}$ ($j \neq 0$) | Generates more training pairs per sentence; excels on rare domain terms. |

In practice, computing a full Softmax over a $100{,}000$-word vocabulary on every step is expensive. Modern implementations speed this up using **Negative Sampling**: instead of updating all $100{,}000$ output rows, the model only contrasts the true context word against $k \approx 5\text{–}20$ randomly sampled negative words.

Why train your own embeddings instead of downloading generic ones? Suppose you are building a classifier for biomedical papers, legal contracts, or software error logs. Generic news-trained embeddings may lump specialized acronyms together or miss domain relationships altogether. Training a lightweight CBoW or Skip-Gram model on your own unlabeled corpus takes just minutes in PyTorch and produces crisp, domain-aligned word vectors where technical synonyms cluster together naturally.

You can also evaluate custom embeddings immediately by checking **nearest neighbors** via Cosine Similarity—confirming that related terms sit close in vector space before plugging the `nn.Embedding` weights into downstream classifiers. Even a small domain-specific corpus of a few thousand sentences is enough to surface meaningful clusters around your core technical vocabulary.

## Hands-On: Sliding-Window CBoW & Skip-Gram Dataset Builder

Let's generate CBoW and Skip-Gram training pairs from a sentence with context window radius $C = 2$ in pure Python.

  expectedOutput="CBoW Pairs (context -> center):
  (['deep', 'learning', 'with', 'pytorch'], 'models')
  (['learning', 'models', 'pytorch', 'rocks'], 'with')
Skip-Gram Pairs (center -> context) for 'models':
  ('models', 'deep')
  ('models', 'learning')
  ('models', 'with')
  ('models', 'pytorch')"
  initialCode={`tokens = "deep learning models with pytorch rocks".split()
window = 2

cbow_pairs = []
skipgram_pairs = []

for idx in range(window, len(tokens) - window):
    center = tokens[idx]
    context = [tokens[i] for i in range(idx - window, idx + window + 1) if i != idx]
    cbow_pairs.append((context, center))
    for ctx_word in context:
        skipgram_pairs.append((center, ctx_word))

print("CBoW Pairs (context -> center):")
for pair in cbow_pairs:
    print(f"  {pair}")

print("Skip-Gram Pairs (center -> context) for 'models':")
for c, ctx in skipgram_pairs:
    if c == "models":
        print(f"  {(c, ctx)}")`}
