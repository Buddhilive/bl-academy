Unlike images, which arrive as clean numerical grids of pixel intensities, human language is discrete, variable-length, and symbolic. Before any neural network can classify a news article, translate a sentence, or answer a question, we must convert text into numerical **tensors**.

---

## Tokenization & Vocabulary Mapping

Computers already store characters as integers under **ASCII** or **UTF-8**. When feeding text to a model, we first split strings into discrete units called **tokens** and map each unique token to an integer ID inside a **vocabulary**:

- **Character-level tokenization**: Small vocabulary ($C \approx 100$), long sequences, and individual characters carry little semantic meaning on their own.
- **Word-level tokenization**: Preserves whole-word semantics, but requires a huge vocabulary ($V \approx 50{,}000+$) where rare words or typos become unknown (`<UNK>`) tokens.
- **Subword tokenization (BPE / WordPiece)**: Modern sweet spot used by BERT and Gemini, splits rare words into meaningful pieces (`"unhappiness"` → `["un", "happiness"]`).

---

## Bag-of-Words (BoW), N-Grams & TF-IDF

When classifying documents (such as categorizing **AG News** articles into *World*, *Sports*, *Business*, or *Sci/Tech*), we often collapse a variable-length sentence into a fixed-size vector of length $V$:

| Representation | How It Works | Strengths & Limitations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bag-of-Words (BoW)** | Sums one-hot word vectors to count how many times each vocabulary word appears. | Fast and simple baseline; loses word order (*"dog bites man"* = *"man bites dog"*). |
| **N-Grams (Bigrams / Trigrams)** | Treats consecutive $N$-word phrases (e.g., `"neural network"` vs `"fishing network"`) as tokens. | Captures local word order, but causes vocabulary size to explode. |
| **TF-IDF** | Scales word frequency by inverse document frequency across the corpus. | Down-weights ubiquitous filler words (`the`, `is`, `and`) and highlights rare, informative terms. |

Mathematically, **Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF)** for term $t$ in document $d$ across $N$ total documents is:

$$\text{TF-IDF}(t, d) = \text{tf}(t, d) \times \log\left(\frac{N}{1 + \text{df}(t)}\right)$$

In plain English: $\text{tf}(t, d)$ is how often word $t$ appears in document $d$, while $\text{df}(t)$ is how many documents in the entire collection contain that word. Dividing total documents $N$ by $1 + \text{df}(t)$ shrinks the score of everyday words like `"the"` (which appear everywhere) toward zero and boosts rare, topic-defining words like `"neural"` or `"markets"`.

While BoW, N-grams, and TF-IDF provide fast, interpretable baselines for topic classification and spam filtering, they treat every word as an independent coordinate and cannot recognize that *"laptop"* and *"notebook"* share similar meaning. To capture true semantics, we'll move to dense word embeddings next.

## Hands-On: Tokenizer, Bigrams & TF-IDF Scorer in Python

Let's build a pure-Python text vectorizer that generates **word unigrams**, **bigrams**, and **TF-IDF scores** across three mini news headlines. Notice how common words like `"the"` score near zero while domain words like `"neural"` or `"markets"` stand out.

**Bigrams & TF-IDF Vectorizer**

```python
import math
from collections import Counter

docs = [
    "the neural network learns",
    "the markets rally",
    "the team wins the game",
]

tokenized = [d.lower().split() for d in docs]
bigrams_0 = list(zip(tokenized[0], tokenized[0][1:]))
print(f"Bigrams for Doc 0: {bigrams_0}")

N = len(docs)
df = Counter()
for words in tokenized:
    for w in set(words):
        df[w] += 1

def tfidf_scores(words: list[str]) -> list[tuple[str, float]]:
    counts = Counter(words)
    scores = {}
    for w, c in counts.items():
        tf = c / len(words)
        idf = math.log(N / df[w])
        scores[w] = round(tf * idf, 4)
    return sorted(scores.items(), key=lambda x: (-x[1], x[0]))

print(f"Doc 0 Top TF-IDF Terms: {tfidf_scores(tokenized[0])}")
print(f"Doc 1 Top TF-IDF Terms: {tfidf_scores(tokenized[1])}")
```

**Expected Output:**

```text
Bigrams for Doc 0: [('the', 'neural'), ('neural', 'network'), ('network', 'learns')]
Doc 0 Top TF-IDF Terms: [('learns', 0.2747), ('network', 0.2747), ('neural', 0.2747), ('the', 0.0)]
Doc 1 Top TF-IDF Terms: [('markets', 0.3662), ('rally', 0.3662), ('the', 0.0)]
```
