## Avoiding Deceptive Charts

Even well-intentioned analysts can mislead readers if they pick the wrong visual encoding. Watch out for these classic red flags:

| Deception Pattern | How It Fools the Eye | Honest Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reversed / Non-Chronological X-Axis** | Ordering dates out of sequence (e.g., sorting bars from tallest to shortest) creates a fake "downward trend" | Always sort time series strictly chronologically left-to-right |
| **Inverted Y-Axis** | Putting `0` at the top and high values at the bottom makes a spike look like a drop | Keep baseline `0` at the bottom and increase upward |
| **Distorted Proportions** | Scaling 2D/3D icons by height instead of area exaggerates tiny differences | Use standard 1D bar lengths starting from zero |
| **Spurious Correlations** | Plotting two unrelated trends on dual axes to imply causation | Test domain plausibility and report statistical context |

## Color, Accessibility & Readability

Color carries cultural and emotional weight (blue evokes trust, red signals urgency or danger, green suggests ecology). Always ensure your custom palettes remain legible for **colorblind users** (preferring palettes like `viridis`, `cividis`, or Okabe-Ito alongside redundant labels or markers). For readability:
- Place long lists of categories (like 50 states) on the **Y-axis** so labels read horizontally.
- Label both axes clearly, include units, and angle dense x-axis ticks only when necessary.
- Use `mpl_toolkits.mplot3d` or interactive libraries like **NetworkX** and **Plotly** when 3D or relational topology adds genuine clarity.
