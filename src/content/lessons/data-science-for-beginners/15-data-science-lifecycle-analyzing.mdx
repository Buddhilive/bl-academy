Once you've captured a dataset, how do you verify it can actually answer your stakeholders' questions before spending days training models? You run **Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)**. EDA is the detective work of the Data Science Lifecycle—profiling distributions, sampling subsets, querying hypotheses, and hunting down missing values.

In this lesson, we'll apply Pandas EDA techniques to the **Email Spam Classification** word-count dataset.

---

## Four Core EDA Techniques in Pandas

| Technique | Pandas Method | What It Uncovers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Data Profiling** | `df.describe()` | Summarizes `count`, `mean`, `std`, `min`, quartiles (`25%`, `50%`, `75%`), and `max` across numeric columns to test sufficiency |
| **Random Sampling** | `df.sample(n=100)` | Extracts a representative random subset for fast prototyping; larger samples yield tighter generalizations |
| **Targeted Querying** | `df.query("free > 2 and prediction == 1")` | Slices rows matching a specific hypothesis (e.g., do spam emails contain `"free"` more than twice?) |
| **Missingness Audit** | `df.isna().sum()` | Counts missing/inconsistent values and prompts you to investigate *why* they went missing before imputing |

Remember: you don't have to wait until cleaning is 100% finished to plot exploratory charts. Quick histograms or bar charts during EDA often surface anomalies that raw summary tables hide.

### Investigating Why Values Go Missing

When `isna()` or `isnull()` flags gaps in your dataset, resist the urge to immediately drop or zero-fill them. Ask *why* the values vanished in the first place:
- **Missing Completely at Random (MCAR)**: A sensor briefly lost Wi-Fi—safe to drop or impute without introducing bias.
- **Missing at Random (MAR)**: Younger respondents skipped an income question—use group-aware imputation rather than a global mean.
- **Missing Not at Random (MNAR)**: High-risk patients dropped out of a follow-up study—dropping those rows silently skews your model's conclusions.

Checking missingness alongside `describe()` and `sample()` ensures your data actually supports the stakeholder goal before modeling begins.

## Try It Yourself: Email Spam EDA Profiler & Query Engine

Run this pure-Python EDA profiler on sample email word-count records to compute `describe()` statistics, execute a targeted `query()`, and measure spam-to-ham word lift ratios.

**Email Spam EDA Profiler & Query**

```python
emails = [
    {"id": 1, "the": 4, "free": 3, "offer": 2, "spam": 1},
    {"id": 2, "the": 7, "free": 0, "offer": 0, "spam": 0},
    {"id": 3, "the": None, "free": 5, "offer": 4, "spam": 1},
    {"id": 4, "the": 5, "free": 0, "offer": 0, "spam": 0},
    {"id": 5, "the": 2, "free": 1, "offer": 0, "spam": 0},
    {"id": 6, "the": 3, "free": 0, "offer": 1, "spam": 0},
]

free_vals = [e["free"] for e in emails]
print("=== Word Count Profile ('free') ===")
print(f"count={len(free_vals)} | mean={sum(free_vals)/len(free_vals):.2f} | min={min(free_vals)} | max={max(free_vals)}")

matched = [e for e in emails if e["free"] >= 2]
spam_rate = sum(e["spam"] for e in matched) / len(matched) * 100
print("\n=== Query: free >= 2 ===")
print(f"Matched {len(matched)} email(s), Spam rate: {spam_rate:.1f}%")

missing_the = sum(1 for e in emails if e["the"] is None)
print("\n=== Missingness Check (isna) ===")
print(f"Missing 'the' counts: {missing_the} record(s) flagged")
```

**Expected Output:**

```text
=== Word Count Profile ('free') ===
count=6 | mean=1.50 | min=0 | max=5

=== Query: free >= 2 ===
Matched 2 email(s), Spam rate: 100.0%

=== Missingness Check (isna) ===
Missing 'the' counts: 1 record(s) flagged
```
