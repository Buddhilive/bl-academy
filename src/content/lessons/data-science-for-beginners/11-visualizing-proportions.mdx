When your dataset is purely categorical—like whether a wild mushroom is **edible** or **poisonous**, or which forest habitat it prefers—you want to show **proportions**: how each slice contributes to the whole. In this lesson, we'll analyze the Audubon gilled mushrooms dataset (8,124 specimens across 23 species) and build **Pie**, **Donut**, and **Waffle** charts.

---

## Preparing Categorical Data in Pandas

Every column in `mushrooms.csv` (`class`, `cap-shape`, `cap-color`, `odor`, `habitat`, and 18 others) starts as text (`object` dtype). Converting those columns to `category` and grouping gives clean counts:

- `Edible`: `4,208` specimens (`51.8%`)
- `Poisonous`: `3,916` specimens (`48.2%`)

Always verify that your `labels` list matches the alphabetical/grouped index order returned by `.groupby(['class']).count()`, otherwise you'll accidentally swap Edible and Poisonous labels on your chart!

When exploring the seven mushroom habitats (`Grasses`, `Leaves`, `Meadows`, `Paths`, `Urban`, `Waste`, `Wood`), grouping by `habitat` reveals that woods and grasses dominate the dataset. Similarly, grouping by `cap-color` exposes nine distinct shades—from common brown and gray caps to rare green and purple specimens—making a 100-square grid far easier to compare than tiny 1% pie wedges. `PyWaffle` even lets you swap plain squares for Font Awesome mushroom icons when presenting to non-technical audiences.

---

## Pie, Donut & Waffle Charts Compared

| Chart Type | How It Works in Matplotlib / PyWaffle | Best Used For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pie Chart** | `plt.pie(counts, labels=labels, autopct='%.1f %%')` | 2–4 high-contrast categories (e.g., Edible vs Poisonous) |
| **Donut Chart** | Pie chart + `plt.Circle((0, 0), 0.40, fc='white')` added via `fig.gca().add_artist(...)` (`pctdistance=0.85`) | Showing proportions with cleaner whitespace for a center title or KPI |
| **Waffle Chart** | `FigureClass=Waffle` in `pywaffle` rendering a 2D grid of colored squares or Font Awesome icons | Comparing 5–10 categories (like 9 mushroom cap colors) with instant visual counting |

## Try It Yourself: Mushroom Proportions & 10x10 Waffle Grid

Run this pure-Python proportion calculator to verify the Edible vs Poisonous split, 7 mushroom habitat percentages, and render a 100-cell ASCII Waffle chart of cap colors.

**Mushroom Proportions & 10x10 Waffle Grid**

```python
edible, poisonous = 4208, 3916
total = edible + poisonous
print("=== Edible vs Poisonous ===")
print(f"Edible: {edible} ({edible / total * 100:.1f}%)")
print(f"Poisonous: {poisonous} ({poisonous / total * 100:.1f}%)")

# 100-square Waffle representation of major cap colors
cap_pcts = [("B", 28), ("G", 23), ("R", 19), ("Y", 14), ("W", 16)]
cells = []
for code, count in cap_pcts:
    cells.extend([code] * count)

print("\n=== 10x10 Cap Color Waffle (B=Brown, G=Gray, R=Red, Y=Yellow, W=White) ===")
for r in range(10):
    print(" ".join(cells[r * 10 : (r + 1) * 10]))
```

**Expected Output:**
