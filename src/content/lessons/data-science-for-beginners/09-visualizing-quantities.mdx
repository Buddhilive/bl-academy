Once your data is clean, the fastest way to understand what is actually inside it is to plot it. Numbers in a 400-row table hide mistakes that jump off the screen in two seconds on a chart. In this lesson, we'll use **Matplotlib** on a dataset of Minnesota birds to visualize quantities, catch absurd data-entry typos, and compare bird categories with horizontal bar charts.

---

## Choosing the Right Chart for Your Question

Before writing plotting code, match your chart type to the story your data needs to tell:

| Analytical Goal | Best Chart Types |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Analyze trends over time** | Line chart |
| **Compare discrete values & categories** | Bar chart, Horizontal Bar, Scatterplot |
| **Show parts of a whole** | Pie, Donut, Waffle chart |
| **Show distribution of values** | Histogram, Box plot, Scatterplot |
| **Show relationships between variables** | Line, Scatterplot, Bubble, Dual-axis |

## Spotting Outliers: The 23-Meter "Pterodactyl" Typo

When we plot `birds['MaxWingspan']` as a basic line chart, a massive spike shoots up to `2300` cm—a 23-meter wingspan! Adding 45-degree x-axis labels for 440+ species turns unreadable fast, so we switch to a **scatter plot** (`'bo'` blue circles), hide the crowded x-axis ticks with `plt.tick_params(..., labelbottom=False)`, and annotate only birds with `MaxWingspan > 500`:

- **Bald eagle** (`2300` cm — clearly an extra `0` typo for `230` cm)
- **Prairie falcon** (`2500` cm — another extra `0` typo)

Filtering out those two mislabeled records immediately reveals the true wingspan scale across Minnesota's wildlife.

---

## Horizontal & Superimposed Bar Charts

Plotting raw ungrouped rows as a vertical bar chart produces an unreadable wall of bars. Instead, we use Pandas to aggregate first (`birds['Category'].value_counts()`) and Matplotlib to render a **horizontal bar chart** (`plot.barh()`):

- **Category Counts**: `Ducks/Geese/Waterfowl` tops the chart—no surprise in Minnesota, the "Land of 10,000 Lakes."
- **Superimposed Min/Max Lengths**: Calling `plt.barh(category, maxLength)` followed by `plt.barh(category, minLength)` overlays minimum and maximum body lengths per category, visually revealing that larger birds also span wider length ranges.

## Try It Yourself: Wingspan Outlier Detector & ASCII Bar Chart

Run this pure-Python bird analyzer to flag the `> 500 cm` wingspan typos, filter them out, and render a horizontal bar chart of category counts and length ranges.

**Bird Wingspan Outlier Filter & Category Bars**

```python
birds = [
    {"Name": "Black-bellied whistling-duck", "Category": "Ducks/Geese/Waterfowl", "MaxWingspan": 94},
    {"Name": "Snow goose", "Category": "Ducks/Geese/Waterfowl", "MaxWingspan": 165},
    {"Name": "Ross's goose", "Category": "Ducks/Geese/Waterfowl", "MaxWingspan": 116},
    {"Name": "Bald eagle", "Category": "Hawks/Eagles", "MaxWingspan": 2300},
    {"Name": "Prairie falcon", "Category": "Falcons", "MaxWingspan": 2500},
    {"Name": "Red-tailed hawk", "Category": "Hawks/Eagles", "MaxWingspan": 133},
    {"Name": "Ruby-throated hummingbird", "Category": "Hummingbirds", "MaxWingspan": 84},
]

print("=== Wingspan Outliers (> 500 cm) ===")
clean = []
for b in birds:
    if b["MaxWingspan"] > 500:
        print(f"FLAGGED: {b['Name']} ({b['MaxWingspan']} cm)")
    else:
        clean.append(b)

avg_ws = sum(b["MaxWingspan"] for b in clean) / len(clean)
print(f"\n=== Clean Max Wingspan Mean ===\n{avg_ws:.1f} cm across {len(clean)} valid species")

counts = {}
for b in clean:
    counts[b["Category"]] = counts.get(b["Category"], 0) + 1

print("\n=== Species Count by Category ===")
for cat, n in sorted(counts.items(), key=lambda x: -x[1]):
    print(f"{cat:<21} | {'#' * n} ({n})")
```

**Expected Output:**

```text
=== Wingspan Outliers (> 500 cm) ===
FLAGGED: Bald eagle (2300 cm)
FLAGGED: Prairie falcon (2500 cm)

=== Clean Max Wingspan Mean ===
118.4 cm across 5 valid species

=== Species Count by Category ===
Ducks/Geese/Waterfowl | ### (3)
Hawks/Eagles          | # (1)
Hummingbirds          | # (1)
```
